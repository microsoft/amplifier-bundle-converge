# Worked example — Surface v1

Contract: [`../../contracts/surface.v1.md`](../../contracts/surface.v1.md)

One intent steward's morning on the phone, from opening the app to putting it
down: the five-item strip, one ratification sheet answered in a pass, and one
fill of the lanes. Eleven minutes, four taps.

> At most five things ask for your word at once. Each arrives worked out, and
> each is answered with a word. The app surfaces; it never interrupts.

## What you land on

Two places — *Direction* and *Operation* — and a short list across the top of
both (clause 1). Re-ranked on every refresh:

```
Needs your word · 5

1  Ratification sheet · 3 small changes to the documents contract   ~2 min
2  Irreversible · delete the old ledger rows before reseeding       ~1 min
3  Only you can check · does the phone layout read at arm's length? ~3 min
4  Priority · two items both claim next; one must wait              ~1 min
5  Later, from yesterday · a proposal on a locked contract         ~4 min
```

On a quiet morning the strip says so, as good news: *Nothing needs your word.*

## The sheet, opened

Several small calls arrive as one sheet, answered in a pass (clause 2). Every
card carries what · why now · the recommendation · at most two trade-offs · the
word to answer with:

```
Sheet · the documents contract · 3 changes

1  Clause 2: "fifty to a hundred lines" → "fifty to a hundred and twenty".
   Why now      three of four contracts run six to nine lines over.
   Recommend    ratify.
   Trade-offs   longer contracts read slower · a limit nobody keeps is
                worse than a looser limit kept.
   Your word    ratified · ratified with edits · declined · later
```

Answering marks the card and shortens the strip. *Later* re-ranks the item and
brings it back; it never dismisses anything (clause 3). Every answer is written
to a dated ratification record — including this one, at 08:14.

Those four words are one of exactly four writes. The others: raise or lower a
priority with a note · drop feedback as text, screenshot, or voice · steer —
objective, budget, lane count, fill the lanes. Each is an operation the manager
session can perform the same way.

## The teammate's proposal

Item 5 is a colleague's pull request; it reads exactly like a manager
session's draft (clause 5): what changes sentence by sentence, the evidence,
what does *not* change. Your questions pin to the paragraphs that raised them.
You answer *later*; the questions travel back where the proposal came from.

Above it, each document shows what changed since you last read it, sentence by
sentence, removals included (clause 6) — so a rewrite is checkable without
rereading it whole.

At the foot of that contract, the lock control sits greyed out, with its four
conditions shown (clause 7):

```
It says what it means                     ✓
It carries a real example, right and wrong ✓
It can be checked against reality          ✓
You have read it and agreed                — not yet

Lock this contract   (unavailable until all four are met)
```

The app never locks anything on its own.

## Operation, and the fill

You switch places. The whole operation is in one view (clause 9): the plan in
order with its reasons, running against waiting on you, truly ready against
waiting on you, each lane's state with drill-in, your limits, the feedback drop.

Done is shown as the evidence itself, in plain words (clause 8) — *Kept · Not
yet · Broken · Pinned open · Can't check* for contracts, *Working · Quiet ·
Silent — may have died* for lanes. Technical detail waits behind a Details fold.

```
Lanes  ██▁▁  2 running of 4 intended      [ Fill the lanes ]
```

One tap. The fill finds two items touching no file the running lanes touch; the
gauge and the board update:

```
Lanes  ████  4 running of 4 intended
```

Stopping something is never a button on this board.

Nothing above holds data of its own (clause 4): it is the project's documents,
code record, queue, lanes, return log, and pending decisions — one copy of the
truth. All of it done one-handed, on a phone, at 390 pixels (clause 10).
